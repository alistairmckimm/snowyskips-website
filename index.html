Loading tips

  • Keep it level with the top. Nothing over the sides or sticking up — we need to carry it safely and legally.
  • Heavy things first, flat things flat. Break down furniture and lay sheets flat to fit more in.
  • Keep steel, concrete and green waste separate where you can — they're cheaper to tip on their own.
  • Mixed loads cost more. Loads that have to be sorted or are rejected at the landfill attract a $380/t + GST charge.
  • Placement. We need firm, flat ground and clear access for the truck. If the bin has to go on a road or footpath, ask us about a council permit.

SOIL, FILL AND VENM

Moving soil around the Monaro? Soil is classed as waste.

We're experts in carting and disposing of virgin excavated natural material (VENM) — the natural clay, gravel, sand, soil and rock that comes out of the ground — in skip bins and on our trucks right across the Snowy Monaro. Under NSW law, excavated soil is classed as waste, even when it's clean. It has to be classified, transported and taken somewhere that can lawfully receive it, with the records to prove it. We do it with the right workflows and full tracking.

How we handle it

What is VENM? VENM stands for virgin excavated natural material: natural clay, gravel, sand, soil or rock dug from ground that hasn't been contaminated by chemicals or industry, and that has no other waste mixed through it.

  • We confirm where the soil is from and what the site was used for
  • Soil generally needs to be tested before it's moved — to confirm it's VENM (or excavated natural material, ENM) and check for contamination
  • Kept clean — never mixed with any other waste
  • Carted in our skip bins or trucks with the right paperwork and docketed records
  • Taken to a landfill or a site that can lawfully accept it
  • Weighed, docketed and photographed — full tracking from your site to its destination

Don't spread weeds

Soil can carry weed seeds and roots. On the Monaro that includes Serrated Tussock and African Lovegrass, to name a couple. Everyone has a duty under the NSW Biosecurity Act to prevent the spread of weeds — so think about weeds when you accept a load and when you have soil removed from your site. Tell us if there are weeds where we're digging or loading, so the soil goes where it should.

Full decontamination on request. We have standard operating procedures for full decontamination of our bins and delivery vehicles. If your property needs it, we can complete this process for an additional charge, with full documentation, so no weeds or seeds come onto your property with our trucks and bins.

Accepting soil or fill? Know where it's from

Free or cheap fill can contain building waste, chemicals, asbestos or weeds. If contaminated fill ends up on your land, you can be left with the clean-up bill — and the penalties for illegal dumping are serious.

Before you accept it, ask: where is it from, what was that site used for, has it been tested and classified, is it weed-free, who is carting it, where is the paperwork — and where is it going?

Had soil delivered by a contractor? Ask for a VENM docket (certificate) for every load — fully filled out and signed — showing where the soil came from, what it is and how much was delivered. Keep it with your property records. No signed paperwork? Don't accept the load.

Not sure? Give us a call, or read the NSW EPA's advice: Free fill can cost you and Snowy Monaro Regional Council's Know your weeds.

Price match — how it works

Found a cheaper skip bin? We'll match it. Just send us the quote before you book and we'll check it.

  • Same job: same bin size (or smaller), same type of waste, same delivery address and a similar hire period.

LANDFILL COSTS

What will my landfill cost? Real loads, real weights.

Every load below is a real Snowy Skips job, photographed at the landfill and weighed on the council weighbridge. The price on each photo is what that load would cost in landfill fees today.

How we charge — the fairest way

Your bin hire price covers the bin, delivery and pickup. Landfill is charged separately, at cost: your load is weighed at the landfill and you pay the council's rate on that weight — nothing added. You get the weighbridge docket, so you're only charged for exactly what you got rid of.

Why not an all-in price?

Some operators build a set weight or a minimum landfill charge into their price. If your load is light — and plenty are — you can end up paying for weight you never used. With us, a light load costs less: a small 1 m³ household load at 0.10 t is about $27 in landfill.

It's the weight, not the size

A 12 m³ load of timber and cabinetry weighed 1.04 t. A 5 m³ load of carpet underlay and synthetic turf weighed 2.2 t. Heavy, wet or dense material costs more — if you're unsure, send us a photo and we'll give you an idea.

Never in a skip bin

Asbestos — never, under any circumstances

It must be removed by a licensed removalist. Homes built or renovated before about 1990 can contain asbestos (fibro sheets, cement roofing and eaves, some vinyl floor backing). It was banned in Australia in 2003. If you think a material might contain it, stop work, don't break, drill or sand it, and get it assessed. Safe Work Australia: asbestos.

Also never

  • Liquids and sludge
  • Food waste
  • Medical waste and sharps
  • Animal carcasses

Please contact us and we can guide you in the right direction to have these correctly and lawfully serviced.

Lithium batteries start fires

Power tools, e-bikes and scooters, vapes, phones, laptops, solar and home batteries can all contain lithium. If one is crushed or damaged it can ignite in the bin, in the truck or at the landfill, putting drivers, landfill staff and your neighbours at risk. Take batteries out of tools and devices, don't load them without asking, and we'll tell you how to dispose of them safely.

What goes where

Goes in the general bin

  • Household clean-outs: furniture, toys, clothing, general junk
  • Renovation waste: timber, plasterboard, carpet and underlay, fittings, laminate, small amounts of tiles and ceramics
  • Builders' general waste: offcuts, insulation, plastics, packaging that isn't cardboard
  • Break furniture down and flatten what you can, so more fits

Keep separate

  • Steel and scrap metal: order a separate steel bin, or lay pieces neatly on top of the load
  • Cardboard: flatten it and keep it out of the general bin. Ask about a recycling bin
  • Green waste: its own green waste bin, with no rubbish or treated timber. Or ask us about hiring a tree chipper
  • Concrete, bricks and soil: very heavy. Ask us about a dedicated bin. Soil is classed as waste — see how we handle soil and VENM

The costly mistake: mixed loads

Mixed loads, such as steel or cardboard mixed through general waste, are rejected at the landfill. We then have to sort the load ourselves, which carries an additional charge of $380 per tonne + GST, on top of the landfill fees. The easy fix: order a separate steel bin, or lay steel neatly on top so the driver can separate it at the landfill. Keep long pieces flat, with no sharp ends sticking out.

Best practice: load it right

  • Tell us what's going in when you order, so we can suggest the right bin.
  • Heavy items first on the bottom, light items on top. Spread the weight evenly.
  • Stay level with or below the top edge. Nothing should stick out. An overloaded bin can't be lifted or driven safely.
  • Putting things on top: steel can go neatly on top of the load. Cardboard and small amounts of concrete or green waste can too, but only if Snowy Skips has approved it first.
  • Keep others from loading your bin. We also hire temporary fencing, portable toilets and other site establishment equipment.
  • Break things down: flatten boxes, take doors off, cut up timber. You'll fit more in.
  • Lift safely: wear gloves and sturdy boots, and get help with heavy items. Safe Work Australia: manual tasks.
  • Clear drop-off spot on firm, level ground with no low wires. On the road or footpath? A council permit is usually required.
  • Sensitive areas: you must tell us if the bin or truck has to be driven over or placed on a sensitive area, such as grass or finished surfaces. We'll work with you on precautions to avoid damage and find the best option.
