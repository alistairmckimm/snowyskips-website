SKIP BIN GUIDE

Loading your skip bin safely

Loading a bin is hard physical work. A few minutes of planning can save you a sore back, a cut hand or an overweight bin. Here's a simple safety checklist for anyone loading their own bin.

1. Before you start

  • Wear the right gear: sturdy closed-in shoes or boots, gloves, long sleeves and long pants. Add safety glasses when you're breaking things up, and a P2 dust mask for dusty jobs like plaster, insulation or old carpet.
  • Check the edges of the bin. Our bins are steel and we quality check every bin before it goes out, but things can get bumped in transit or on site. Before you start, run your eye over the top edges and sides where you'll be loading, and look for any sharp edges, burrs or damage. If you find anything, don't load over that spot, and give us a call.
  • Keep kids and pets away from the bin and the work area. Never let children play in or around a skip bin.
  • Clear a path from your pile to the bin: no hoses, loose boards or trip hazards, and watch for wet, icy or uneven ground.
  • Check the weather. On hot days start early, take breaks, drink plenty of water and wear sunscreen and a hat. In wind, keep light material like insulation and plastic down low in the bin. In frost and snow, take extra care with your footing.
  • Sort before you load: heavy from light, long from short, and anything that can't go in the bin set aside.
  • Know what can't go in: hot ashes, batteries, gas bottles, chemicals, paint and asbestos. See what can and can't go in a skip bin, or ask us if you're not sure.

2. Lift safely

Most loading injuries are backs and shoulders, usually from lifting something too heavy or awkward, or twisting while you lift it over the side of the bin.

  • Test it first. Tip one corner of the item to feel the weight. If it's too heavy, too long or awkward, get help or break it down.
  • Get close and set your feet about shoulder-width apart, bend your knees, keep your back straight and lift with your legs.
  • Keep the load close to your body, between your knees and shoulders where you can.
  • Don't twist. Turn by moving your feet, not your back.
  • Lifting over the side of the bin is the hardest part. Don't lift heavy items above shoulder height. Use two people for anything heavy or awkward, like lounges, fridges, mattresses and full sheets of ply or plasterboard.
  • Use a wheelbarrow, trolley or sack truck to move things to the bin instead of carrying them.
  • Pace yourself. Take regular breaks, swap jobs around, and stop if anything hurts.

3. Load the bin

  • Heavy and flat items first, spread evenly across the floor of the bin.
  • Break it down: take furniture apart, flatten boxes and cut long timber to fit, then fill the gaps with smaller items.
  • Load level with the top of the bin. Nothing sticking out or piled above the sides, or we can't safely lift it and carry it on the road.
  • Heavy waste fills up by weight, not space. Concrete, bricks, tiles and soil get heavy very quickly. Only fill to the fill mark we give you, and don't top up a heavy bin with more heavy waste.
  • Watch for sharps: nails, screws, broken glass and sheet metal edges. Hammer over nails sticking out of timber, or load it nail-side down.
  • Don't climb into the bin or stand on its sides, and don't build ramps from loose planks.
  • Don't throw things in from a height, a balcony or a ladder, and stay clear of the bin while someone else is loading it.
  • Cover it if rain is coming. Wet waste is heavier, and a tarp keeps light material from blowing out.

4. On pickup day

  • Leave clear access for the truck: move cars, trailers and anything parked near the bin.
  • Keep everyone, especially kids and pets, well clear while the truck is lifting the bin.
  • If something went in that you're unsure about, tell the driver or give us a call before pickup.

Not sure, or not up to it?

If you have a sore back, an injury or a health condition that makes lifting risky, don't push through it. Get someone else to do the lifting, or let our crew load it for you. Not sure about an item? Text us a photo or call 0408 632 007.

This is general safety information to help you load your bin, not professional advice for your situation.
