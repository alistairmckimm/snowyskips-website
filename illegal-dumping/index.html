Stop illegal dumping

Illegal dumping costs ratepayers, spoils our bush, rivers and roadsides, and gives the whole waste industry a bad name. If you see it, report it. And before you pay anyone to take your rubbish away, make sure you know where it's going.

Report illegal dumping

Seen someone dumping, or found a pile of waste where it shouldn't be? Don't approach anyone and don't touch the waste. If it's safe, note the date, time and exact location, the type of waste, and any vehicle details or number plate, and take photos. Then report it:

Why this matters to us

Across the waste industry, and especially in the cities, a small number of operators have given everyone a bad name. Instead of paying to dispose of waste properly, they dump it on roadsides, in the bush or on someone's farm, and pocket the difference. Honest operators get undercut, councils and ratepayers pay for the clean-up, and the damage to land and waterways can last for years.

Regulators are cracking down. In 2026 EPA Victoria started a snap inspection blitz on skip bin operators, and in an earlier blitz in Victoria's south-west, more than half the sites inspected didn't have the required EPA permissions to operate (EPA Victoria). EPA Victoria also urges people to keep an eye on where their skip bin loads of renovation waste and hard rubbish end up.

Is it happening near you?

Illegal dumping isn't as common in the Snowy Monaro as it is around the cities, but it does happen here, and once a spot starts getting dumped on it tends to keep happening. If waste is turning up on your property, a back road or a gateway near you:

  • Report every load, even small ones. Reports help council and the EPA spot hot spots and patterns.
  • Consider putting up a surveillance or trail camera on your own property, covering gateways, laneways and spots that get hit. Footage of a vehicle and number plate is often what it takes to catch the people responsible and get them out of the industry. In the Badgerys Creek case above, CCTV footage helped confirm who did it. Check the rules on camera use in NSW before you set one up, and don't record into neighbours' private spaces.
  • Lock gates and light up problem spots where you can.
  • Never confront a dumper. Note what you can from a safe distance and report it.

You can be on the hook too

In NSW, if your waste is taken somewhere it can't lawfully go, it isn't only the driver who is in trouble. Under the law, the owner of the waste, the owner of the vehicle and the transporter can each be guilty of an offence (NSW EPA). That's why it pays to know exactly where your rubbish is going.

How to pick an honest operator

The most important question: do they have a full waste workflow? An honest operator can show you exactly where your waste goes, from the moment it leaves your property to the weighbridge at the landfill, transfer station or recycler, with the paperwork to prove it. Without that, "we dispose of it responsibly" can be very misleading. Your waste could end up anywhere, and as the owner you could still be liable.

  • Ask where your waste is going, and expect a straight answer: a named landfill, transfer station or recycler, not just "the tip".
  • Ask how they track it: a record of each load, its weight, where it went and when.
  • Ask for the weighbridge docket or a disposal record for your load.
  • Be wary of prices that seem too good to be true. Landfill fees are real costs. If a quote looks too cheap to cover them, ask how.
  • Check they're a real business: an ABN, a physical address, and a name and phone number you can call back.
  • Watch out for cash-only, no-paperwork deals and operators who turn up in unmarked trailers.

How we do it

We want to help lift the standard of the industry, starting with full transparency on every load.

  • Every load goes to a proper disposal facility, and recyclables like scrap steel, green waste and concrete go where they can be reused.
  • Every load is weighed at the landfill, and you get the docket. Landfill is charged at cost from that docket, so there's no reason to cut corners. See how we charge.
