Loading tips

  • Keep it level with the top. Nothing over the sides or sticking up — we need to carry it safely and legally.
  • Heavy things first, flat things flat. Break down furniture and lay sheets flat to fit more in.
  • Keep steel, concrete and green waste separate where you can — they're cheaper to tip on their own.
  • Mixed loads cost more. Loads that have to be sorted or are rejected at the landfill attract a $380/t + GST charge.
  • Placement. We need firm, flat ground and clear access for the truck. If the bin has to go on a road or footpath, ask us about a council permit.

SOIL, FILL AND VENM

Moving soil around the Monaro? Soil is classed as waste.

We're experts in carting and disposing of virgin excavated natural material (VENM) — the natural clay, gravel, sand, soil and rock that comes out of the ground — in skip bins and on our trucks right across the Snowy Monaro. Under NSW law, excavated soil is classed as waste, even when it's clean. It has to be classified, transported and taken somewhere that can lawfully receive it, with the records to prove it. We do it with the right workflows and full tracking.

How we handle it

What is VENM? VENM stands for virgin excavated natural material: natural clay, gravel, sand, soil or rock dug from ground that hasn't been contaminated by chemicals or industry, and that has no other waste mixed through it.

  • We confirm where the soil is from and what the site was used for
  • Soil generally needs to be tested before it's moved — to confirm it's VENM (or excavated natural material, ENM) and check for contamination
  • Kept clean — never mixed with any other waste
  • Carted in our skip bins or trucks with the right paperwork and docketed records
  • Taken to a landfill or a site that can lawfully accept it
  • Weighed, docketed and photographed — full tracking from your site to its destination

Don't spread weeds

Soil can carry weed seeds and roots. On the Monaro that includes Serrated Tussock and African Lovegrass, to name a couple. Everyone has a duty under the NSW Biosecurity Act to prevent the spread of weeds — so think about weeds when you accept a load and when you have soil removed from your site. Tell us if there are weeds where we're digging or loading, so the soil goes where it should.

Full decontamination on request. We have standard operating procedures for full decontamination of our bins, equipment and delivery vehicles. If your property needs it, we can complete this process for an additional charge, with full documentation, so no weeds or seeds come onto your property with our trucks, bins or machinery.

Moving soil? Know your weeds: what to look for

Weed seeds and roots spread very easily in soil, fill, mulch and green waste, so every load of material shifted around the region can carry weeds to a new paddock, roadside or backyard. A lot of people don't know what these look like until they're everywhere. These are some of the most common invasive weeds on the Monaro, plus orange hawkweed, a high-country weed that must be reported. If you have them where we're digging or loading, let us know before we start.

Not sure what you've got? Ask a biosecurity officer. Snowy Monaro Regional Council's biosecurity officers can help you identify weeds from photos, or arrange a free visit to your property, and explain what you need to do about them.

Accepting soil or fill? Know where it's from

Free or cheap fill can contain building waste, chemicals, asbestos or weeds. If contaminated fill ends up on your land, you can be left with the clean-up bill — and the penalties for illegal dumping are serious.

Before you accept it, ask: where is it from, what was that site used for, has it been tested and classified, is it weed-free, who is carting it, where is the paperwork — and where is it going?

Had soil delivered by a contractor? Ask for a VENM docket (certificate) for every load — fully filled out and signed — showing where the soil came from, what it is and how much was delivered. Keep it with your property records. No signed paperwork? Don't accept the load.

Not sure? Give us a call, or read the NSW EPA's advice: Free fill can cost you and Snowy Monaro Regional Council's Know your weeds.
